HIP-0007: Active Inference — Local Beliefs, World Models and Planning

Abstract

This HIP specifies Hanzo's active-inference primitive: maintain beliefs about a partially observed world, predict consequences, and infer policies that balance preferred outcomes with information gain. Sekai models the world; Kai decides; Enso routes; Zen generates; deterministic policy governs. World modeling and planning use /v1/worlds; bounded judgments remain at /v1/decisions.

The mathematical reference is the hanzo-active-inference crate in hanzoai/ml, with the native host boundary hanzo-worlds in hanzoai/engine. The categorical core implements exact finite-state inference and finite-horizon open-loop planning. Sparse world-state scheduling, learned latent dynamics, capability adaptation and domain qualification are separate conformance profiles. A runtime MUST declare which profiles it actually implements.

Final settles this specification; it does not assert that every profile is implemented, that a crate has been published, or that an application is qualified for physical or business execution. The implementation matrix in §1 is part of the specification, not an implied deployment claim.

Motivation

An agent usually needs a sufficient local belief for a decision, not an exact reconstruction of the entire world. Re-encoding all observations and updating all entities after each event wastes compute and hides what is unknown. Treating a plausible generated explanation as a belief update also loses the distinction between an observation, a prediction and an authorized action.

The primitive must therefore support bounded local work, missing modalities, uncertainty, prediction error and explicit abstention. It must make information seeking mathematically meaningful, preserve the evidence behind a decision, and specialize cheaply without giving a learned model authority over safety or money.

Specification

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY are interpreted as in RFC 2119 and RFC 8174 when capitalized. Logarithms are natural; information and free-energy terms are in nats unless a response declares another unit.

§3 Generative model and belief state

For a categorical local factor, let s denote state, o outcome, a action and pi an explicitly enumerated finite action sequence. Let:

A[a][o][s]       = P(o | s, a)              observation likelihood
B[a][s_next][s]  = P(s_next | s, a)        controlled transition
C[o]            = P_preferred(o)           preference distribution
D[s]            = P(s_initial)            initial belief
E[pi]           = P_prior(pi)              habit/policy prior
q[s]            = Q(s)                    current belief
gamma           >= 0                      policy precision

Every A and B column MUST be finite, nonnegative and sum to one within a declared numerical tolerance. Beliefs and E MUST be normalized. C MUST be strictly positive and normalized in categorical-core-v1; structural prohibitions belong in hard masks, not log(0) preference tricks. Actions, states, outcomes and policies MUST share a registered schema and stable ordering. Ragged arrays, unknown indices, negative probabilities and non-finite inputs MUST be refused.

A model manifest MUST distinguish the generative model from the belief it is currently applied to. It MUST identify the state/outcome/action schema and units, likelihood/transition revisions, preference revision and supported horizons. A generative parameter change, a preference change and new observational evidence are distinct revisions; none may be hidden as an ordinary event replay.

The reference core limits one factor to 256 states, 256 outcomes and 64 actions, and a call to 256 supplied policies with horizon at most 16. Its normalization tolerance is 1e-9. These are implementation limits, not mathematical constants; other limits MUST be advertised and enforced before unbounded work is allocated.

§4 Prediction, inference and variational free energy

Prediction is separate from conditioning on a current observation:

q_pred(s_next) = sum_s B[a][s_next][s] q(s)
Z(o,a)        = sum_s A[a][o][s] q_prior(s)
q_post(s)     = A[a][o][s] q_prior(s) / Z(o,a)

observe MUST NOT apply a hidden extra transition. A caller observing the result of an action first predicts through B, then conditions through A. A current-time measurement conditions its current prior directly. The host MUST distinguish these two cases in its event/action schema.

For a candidate posterior Q, the variational free energy is

F(Q) = sum_s Q(s) [log Q(s) - log q_prior(s) - log A[a][o][s]]
     = KL(Q || P(s | o,a)) - log P(o | a).

At the exact Bayesian posterior, F* = -log Z. A zero-mass Q term contributes zero; positive Q mass outside the joint support has infinite F. A wire response MUST represent that condition as a typed support error, not non-finite JSON. A zero-evidence observation MUST fail or enter an explicitly declared robust outlier model. It MUST NOT be silently normalized into a confident belief.

Bayesian posterior updates MAY happen on each observation. This does not authorize production gradient updates to encoders or learned dynamics (§11). Approximate inference MUST name its approximation, convergence criterion and residual work.

§5 Expected free energy and policy inference

For each future step of a supplied policy, predict q_pi(s) through its selected transition and form q_pi(o) = sum_s A[a][o][s] q_pi(s). The categorical profile minimizes:

risk        = KL(q_pi(o) || C(o))
ambiguity   = sum_s q_pi(s) H(A[a][:][s])
extrinsic   = -sum_o q_pi(o) log C(o)
information_gain = H(q_pi(o)) - ambiguity
G_step      = risk + ambiguity
            = extrinsic - information_gain
G(pi)       = sum_future_steps G_step(pi).

Positive information gain reduces the cost being minimized. An implementation MUST NOT add positive curiosity as a penalty while claiming this objective. It MUST return both decompositions and verify their equality within a declared tolerance. Expected observation ambiguity, state information gain, parameter information gain and ensemble variance MUST NOT share an unlabeled field.

For nonnegative finite precision, policy inference is

Q(pi) = normalize(allowed(pi) * E(pi) * exp(-gamma * G(pi))).

Normalization MUST be numerically stable. A disallowed policy has exactly zero posterior mass. If no allowed policy has positive prior, the result is defer or a typed refusal; there is no fallback to an unauthorized action. At gamma zero, Q reduces to the prior restricted to allowed policies. Selection MUST have a specified tie rule; the reference chooses the first maximum. A response MUST retain the selected candidate's own terms, not the last candidate evaluated.

The step terms are exact for the declared predictive marginals. Their open-loop sum does not condition on every possible future observation history and is not the joint information gain of the entire future trajectory.

The profile is open-loop: each supplied action sequence is evaluated without a future observation-contingent branch. Its sum is not a full closed-loop policy tree. A closed-loop extension MUST represent future observations and conditional actions explicitly and state its pruning/approximation. It MUST NOT repeatedly count the same evidence as independent information gain. Unequal horizons MUST be declared; equal-length candidates SHOULD be used when comparing total scores.

The public information-seeking fixture has q=[0.5,0.5], identity B for both candidate actions, C=[0.5,0.5], E=[0.5,0.5] and gamma 4. Action 0 has identity A; action 1 has every A entry 0.5. Its expected values are:

| Action | Risk | Ambiguity | Extrinsic | Information gain | G | Policy probability | |---|---|---|---|---|---|---| | Informative | 0 | 0 | ln(2) | ln(2) | 0 | 16/17 | | Uninformative | 0 | ln(2) | ln(2) | 0 | ln(2) | 1/17 |

Masking the informative action MUST give it probability zero and the other action probability one. Masking both MUST refuse. Conditioning the identity likelihood on an impossible outcome MUST refuse. These fixtures test epistemic behavior and safety independently of any model checkpoint or language understanding.

A parameter-epistemic extension MAY maintain a posterior over parameters or model hypotheses theta. It MUST declare the joint predictive distribution, posterior update and estimator. A consistent joint objective is

G_extended = -E_q(o|pi) log C(o) - I_q((s,theta); o | pi)
I_q((s,theta); o | pi) = I_q(theta; o | pi)
                       + E_q(theta) I_q(s; o | theta, pi).

The parameter term is E_q(o|pi) KL(q(theta|o,pi) || q(theta)): posterior relative to prior after an outcome. An implementation MUST NOT add unconditional state and parameter information gains as if they were independent; their shared information can be double-counted. With fixed theta this reduces to the categorical state term. The core MUST NOT claim parameter information gain from state information gain alone. Cost, latency, energy or privacy penalties MAY be added as a separately named objective with declared units and weights. The response MUST preserve G and the additional terms separately; the result MUST NOT be labeled unmodified EFE.

§6 Learning categorical parameters

Positive Dirichlet pseudo-counts alpha[a][o][s] and beta[a][s_next][s] define A and B by column normalization. For a known previous belief, action and resulting observation, compute the adjacent-state joint:

xi(s_next,s) proportional to
    q_previous(s) B[a][s_next][s] A[a][o][s_next]
q_post(s_next) = sum_s xi(s_next,s)
alpha_new[a][o][s_next] = alpha_old[a][o][s_next] + eta q_post(s_next)
beta_new[a][s_next][s]  = beta_old[a][s_next][s] + eta xi(s_next,s).

The learning rate eta MUST be finite and positive. A failed update MUST leave the old value unchanged; normalized tables and counts MUST remain finite and valid. The added total mass to each selected count tensor is eta. Unselected actions MUST remain unchanged. This is an expected-count update using the current model; it is not a claim of globally exact structure learning or parameter planning.

C and hard constraints MUST NOT be learned from the frequency of observed harmful outcomes. Preference learning, when enabled by application policy, MUST be versioned separately from non-negotiable constraints and retain consent/provenance.

§7 Sparse event-driven world runtime

A world is an authenticated namespace for a versioned generative model, entity and factor identities, timestamped beliefs, observations/actions and derivation references. A world implementation MUST keep observation time distinct from ingest time and model/graph validity time. Evidence retains source, units, frame, quality, missingness, sensor/encoder revision and allowed use where applicable.

An observation MUST NOT be stored as an established fact merely because an extractor produced it. Predicted relations and counterfactuals remain hypotheses, with the supporting evidence and uncertainty retained. Contradictory observations MUST remain distinguishable; averaging them MUST NOT erase their provenance.

Each accepted event MUST have a tenant/world-scoped idempotency key and a digest. Replaying the same key and content returns the original result. Reusing the key with different content is a conflict. State and deduplication records MUST commit atomically. A late event MUST follow a declared replay, historical-snapshot or refusal policy; it MUST NOT silently replace a newer live belief.

Only affected factors, their necessary separators and decision-dependent readers SHOULD be scheduled. Expensive encoders SHOULD run once per changed content and representation revision; feature caches MUST include tenant and data-use scope. A transition cache MUST also include action, model revision and relevant time.

The scheduler MUST enforce finite node/edge/message/iteration/time budgets and return updated ids, invalidated ids, deferred work, snapshot revision and whether the response is complete. A bound in neighborhood size is not a bound in whole world size: indexed lookups and dependency maintenance costs MUST be measured. No implementation may claim global consistency from a truncated local update.

Invalidation and recomputation are distinct operations. A persistent queue MUST retain deferred work with a resumable cursor or deterministic replay path. A priority function MAY use surprise, belief uncertainty, causal reach, decision relevance and deadline. It MUST account for queue age so unrelated traffic cannot starve a safety-relevant dependency indefinitely. Concurrent updates MUST use a snapshot or revision check; a plan cannot mix incompatible factor revisions.

Loops require a declared message schedule, damping/convergence test and a residual bound or incompleteness marker. Exact inference on one factor does not establish exactness on a loopy graph. A plan depending on stale or deferred factors MUST report them and defer where its application gate requires complete evidence.

Graph identity and derivation use HIP-0522; extracted assertions use HIP-0526. The world runtime SHOULD keep posterior/model state and references to those sources, not create another authoritative document or audit store.

§8 World API and native host contract

All world operations are under /v1/worlds, with model selection in data or registered world configuration. There is no model-branded URL prefix. The host MUST authenticate and resolve tenant/world identity before native inference.

| Method and path | Contract | Experimental reference boundary | |---|---|---| | POST /v1/worlds | Create a tenant-owned world bound to a registered model and domain | Implemented for declared fixtures | | GET /v1/worlds/{id} | Read world identity and model/domain metadata | Implemented subset | | POST /v1/worlds/{id}/events | Ingest one bounded observation/action event and return affected belief state | Observation fixture implemented; generic action/time schema and queue completion are incomplete | | GET /v1/worlds/{id}/beliefs/{entity} | Read belief and provenance at a stated revision | Current fixture belief implemented; historical snapshots are not established | | POST /v1/worlds/{id}/rollouts | Predict consequences without executing actions | Approximate latent fixture implemented | | POST /v1/worlds/{id}/plans | Rank candidate plans under the declared objective and external constraints | Gaussian surrogate fixture implemented; not exact EFE | | POST /v1/worlds/{id}/inference | Filter/plan through the registered categorical host model | Two-state simulation bridge implemented; general native host library exists |

The table defines operation ownership, not a promise that every deployment serves all rows. A deployment MUST publish a versioned schema describing exactly its supported fields, limits and errors. A client MUST NOT send a generic-profile field to a fixture server and assume that it was used. Unknown control fields MUST be rejected; unsupported operations MUST be explicit.

The native WorldInference::infer call takes belief, optional current observation (likelihood_action, outcome), candidate action sequences, and host-supplied habits/admissibility. Its return includes an optional filtered posterior, policy probabilities, selected index, EFE terms and execution_authorized: false. The reference HTTP fixture currently exposes belief and policies only; callers MUST NOT infer support for native observation filtering from the HTTP route name.

Example for the declared two-state simulation fixture only:

{"belief": [0.8, 0.2], "policies": [[0], [1]]}

Its registered actions hold or swap state; its preference distribution is [0.9, 0.1]. The caller supplies neither A/B/C nor an execution authorization. A generic world registry, action schema, preference revision and artifact installation are host integrations, not undocumented extra request fields.

Errors MUST distinguish malformed input, unsupported profile, unknown or unauthorized world, conflicting event/revision, zero evidence, exhausted budget, unavailable backend and no admissible policy. Cross-tenant unknown and forbidden world lookups SHOULD have indistinguishable external responses. A shared-key, trusted-header development server is not a multi-tenant authentication service. The unversioned /plan/efe, /trace/explain and /interop/hstp/send examples are not alternative public routes specified by this HIP.

§9 Traces, explanations and hierarchical control

A world-runtime plan trace MUST bind its tenant, request, world/snapshot revision, model/representation/calibration/preference revisions, observed evidence, declared objective, policy prior/precision, admissibility decisions, candidate terms, selected candidate, budgets, incomplete dependencies and execution mode. A library returning mathematical terms alone does not implement this trace profile.

A counterfactual MUST identify its intervention, starting snapshot, generative model, horizon, predicted outcome and uncertainty. An alternative that was not rolled out is an untested hypothesis, not a measured counterfactual. Zen MAY explain computed traces but MUST NOT invent scores, flip conditions or solver verdicts. Explanations and source data MUST obey the same access/retention policy. Decision Packages and refresh semantics compose with HIP-1332; derivation edges compose with HIP-0522 instead of introducing another provenance subsystem.

Hierarchical control MAY compose macro subgoals with local micro plans. Each level MUST declare its action abstraction, clock/horizon, termination condition, remaining resource budget and revision bindings. Replanning occurs on changed evidence or violated assumptions. An abstract macro action MUST resolve through the same deterministic constraints and executor as its concrete micro actions. No level inherits permission merely because its parent selected a subgoal.

A plan MUST be treated as shadow, advisory or eligible-for-external-authorization. Even the last mode is not execution. Policy joins on ALLOW < ASK < DENY as in HIP-1332; a learned score MUST NOT weaken a denial. Enso MAY allocate additional compute or sensing only among authorized candidates within the remaining budget. Currency, latency and privacy ceilings MUST be enforced as hard admission limits where policy requires them, not merely as costs in a learned score.

When routing uses market prices, the trace MUST bind the price/quote revision, units and expiry separately from G. A router MAY minimize a declared score such as G + lambda_cost estimated_cost + lambda_latency estimated_latency over admissible providers. Exchange rates between nats, currency and time are policy configuration, not a derived physical identity. Market supply/demand and price updates remain the market's responsibility (HIP-0008); planning does not create spending authority or replace its settlement/accounting rules.

§10 Multimodal and predictive latent world models

A modality adapter MUST bind raw schema, units, timestamps, frame and encoder revision to a declared representation. Image, video, audio, text, telemetry, spatial observations, tables and graph events MAY be supported independently. Missingness is an input condition, not a synthetic zero-valued observation. Embeddings from unrelated encoders MUST NOT be assumed aligned because their vectors have been truncated to the same length.

A latent world model SHOULD predict action-conditioned future representations and observation likelihoods on the decision-relevant subgraph. Its manifest MUST state whether it is learned from observations only, actions/outcomes, supervised simulator state, or a combination. The training objective MUST distinguish representation learning, transition prediction, reconstruction and calibration. A joint-embedding objective MUST specify how representation collapse is prevented.

Frozen encoders plus small modality/dynamics adapters SHOULD be the first cost-controlled baseline. Larger unified encoders or video teachers require a measured gain on the same held-out decision tasks. Encoder caches, event-triggered updates and lower-dimensional local predictors SHOULD be benchmarked before continuous global reconstruction is introduced.

A distributional ensemble MAY estimate aleatoric variation and epistemic uncertainty separately. Ensemble disagreement is an estimator, not proof of calibration. A continuous implementation MUST state covariance structure, propagation method, process noise and observation likelihood. Diagonal variance accumulation without Jacobian transport MUST be labeled approximate.

The Gaussian objective distance-to-target + variance + cost is a risk surrogate. It MUST NOT be labeled EFE unless a declared generative/recognition model derives the claimed extrinsic and epistemic terms. Continuous variational/Laplace, particle or Hamiltonian Monte Carlo methods MAY be independent backends; exact categorical filtering does not require Hamiltonian dynamics or a language model.

Object-centric mixture models, Bayesian model reduction and renormalizing hierarchies are research-backed options (§15). A small MLP regime head alone is not evidence of any of those algorithms. Structure growth/pruning MUST retain model-selection evidence and test forgetting, transfer, locality and data cost.

A structure-learning extension SHOULD update sufficient statistics only for the affected object/relation hypotheses, grow a model on calibrated novelty or a specified evidence criterion, and prune/merge by a declared model-evidence or complexity criterion. It MUST retain the prior, evidence contribution, candidate model revisions and decision-relevant regressions. It MUST NOT claim Bayesian model reduction from arbitrary parameter deletion or a fixed-size neural adapter.

A renormalizing hierarchy MUST declare its spatial grouping, state/path abstraction, temporal scale and mappings between levels. Coarse predictions MUST be evaluated against the fine-level outcomes they summarize; macro actions must remain realizable by admissible lower-level actions. Changes to these mappings invalidate dependent caches and plans. These are extension requirements, not algorithms already implemented by the finite-state core or Gaussian fixture.

§11 Capability specialization and training

A capability SHOULD adapt the smallest sufficient part of a pinned base: readout/calibration first, then a transition/observation adapter or low-rank update when target evaluations justify it. A checkpoint per user is not required. The artifact MUST bind tenant/scope, route/question or dynamics role, base digest, representation/schema revision, artifact ABI, calibration, training provenance, protected suites and evaluation/gate references. A mismatch MUST refuse loading; one tenant's capability MUST NOT answer another tenant's request.

Observation-driven posterior or categorical count updates (§4, §6) differ from neural gradient learning. Production request handling MUST NOT synchronously change encoder or dynamics weights. Eligible captures go to a purpose/consent- controlled buffer, then offline training, evaluation, shadow and controlled promotion under HIP-1332, HIP-1333 and HIP-1334. This is the applicable integration rule even where a personalization example illustrates immediate adaptation.

For readout training, captures MUST name the exact unfused feature representation; a posterior that has fused earlier observations is not interchangeable with an encoder feature from one event. Dataset splits MUST separate entire relevant trajectories, entities, users or time blocks before feature extraction/fitting. Calibration and thresholds use validation only. Test/evaluation-only material MUST NOT be used for training, curriculum selection or prompt optimization.

Managed jobs use /v1/train, never a parallel /v1/worlds/train control plane. A world-model backend MUST register supported objectives and adaptation modes and reject unsupported jobs. This HIP does not assert that a particular managed executor already supports latent-world objectives. Artifact publication MUST pass prospectively frozen target and preservation gates from HIP-1334. Neither a successful optimizer exit nor preservation alone establishes target improvement.

A content digest detects a changed artifact; it is not an authentication signature. Distributed tensor artifacts MUST use the safe format required by HIP-0019, with immutable manifests and host-controlled locations. A local weights-only research checkpoint is not a claim of deployment-format conformance. Applying a capability MUST NOT change policy, permissions or preference constraints.

§12 Evaluation, compute and claims

Every claimed result MUST have a reproducible command/configuration, source and artifact revisions, data/split lineage, environment, seed, measured resource use and raw per-item outcomes. Research objects and claims follow HIP-1334. Synthetic fixtures, supplied exact models, inferred observation state and real-world data MUST be separate conditions. A source-review test is not a released-service test.

| Area | Required measurements or checks | |---|---| | Mathematics | VFE bound/tightness, both EFE identities, informative-action fixture, policy normalization/masks, impossible observations, invalid shapes and count conservation | | Prediction | Held-out one/multi-step error, predictive NLL, coverage and calibration, persistence and cheap local baselines | | Perception | True-state versus encoded-observation results; missing/noisy/conflicting modality and stale-sensor slices | | Decisions | Accuracy, per-class precision/recall and support, expected regret, constraint violations, defer/coverage and outcome calibration | | Information seeking | Matched pragmatic-cost cases where information matters; state and parameter epistemic ablations kept separate | | Locality | Nodes/edges/messages touched, queue age, deferred work, residuals, consistency and cost versus a full-update reference | | Runtime | Cold/warm/cache-hit conditions, p50/p95/p99, concurrency/queueing, memory, device/precision and CPU/GPU or energy cost | | Adaptation | Target gain, protected-capability regression, calibration, cross-regime transfer, forgetting and rollback | | Trace | Selected-score consistency, source/revision completeness, replay and computed counterfactual fidelity | | Domain | Task-specific safety constraints, authorization refusals, OOD behavior and fail-safe operation |

A Kai comparison MUST identify its actual model revision and distinguish HTTP request latency from an in-process kernel call. A supplied-model planner and a language reader given rules in text have different information/representation conditions; their score is a task result, not general decision superiority. Cache reuse, option order/labels, warmup, compute budgets and failure handling MUST be recorded. Undefined precision when no positives were predicted MUST be reported with predicted support, not presented as evidence of zero false positives.

A comparison SHOULD include learned world model alone, Kai alone, and composed world-model-plus-Kai under matched evidence and compute ceilings. Confidence intervals or uncertainty estimates SHOULD respect trajectory/entity dependence. Numerical equality across devices MUST be tested rather than inferred from a fixed seed. An exactness claim MUST identify what is bit-identical and what merely falls within a tolerance.

Compute starts with a bounded local smoke run and a profiled pilot. CPU is a sufficient reference for the categorical core. A large GPU allocation, a fixed multi-GPU training duration or expected percentage gain is not a requirement or a result. A job MUST enforce its step/time/spend cap and record actual completion. The smoke run MUST produce an artifact and independent held-out evaluation; training MUST NOT be reported complete merely because a job was submitted.

§13 Domain authorization and failure boundaries

A mathematical or synthetic profile grants no execution authority. The reference native response always has execution_authorized: false. Experimental drone and business worlds MAY ingest observations and predict, but MUST NOT use an unqualified fixture to authorize physical or business actions.

For drones/robots, a domain-qualified executor MUST independently enforce sensor freshness, coordinate/frame validity, flight/operating envelope, collision and geofence constraints, communications-loss handling and a deterministic fail-safe. A plan cannot override operator control or directly drive an actuator through the world inference API. The qualification report MUST state its simulated and real operating conditions and explicit out-of-domain behavior.

For businesses, the executor MUST enforce identity, role, consent, spending and transaction limits, suppression/opt-outs where applicable, approval requirements, idempotency and audit/compensation behavior. A predicted benefit is not permission to send a message, place an order, move money or change an account. Irreversible actions require the authorization the application policy specifies.

A candidate needing unauthorized sensing is inadmissible even if its information gain is high. Loss of evidence, all-masked policies, exhausted budgets, stale revision bindings and backend failure MUST lead to a declared defer/refusal or qualified fail-safe, never fabricated certainty or implicit execution.

§14 Interoperability and release conformance

IEEE 2874-2025 Spatial Web interoperability MAY be implemented as a negotiated adapter over authenticated world/entity/event references. Such an adapter MUST name the exact standard/profile, schema version, identity/credential mapping and conformance tests. A YAML object with spatial labels does not establish HSML/HSTP conformance. TEE, differential privacy, encrypted computation and signatures each require their own stated threat model and evidence; none substitutes for the others. This HIP requires no external interoperability server to run its core.

A release declaration MUST include supported profiles and revisions, public conformance fixtures, local reproduction commands, known limits and qualification scope. Native core, HTTP mounting, artifact publication and domain authorization are separately verifiable release gates. Publishing a math crate does not mount a service, and deploying a service does not qualify an application.

The native release order is canonical ML math crate, then engine consumer against the published dependency, then host/schema integration and its profile tests. A coordinated local Cargo source patch is valid source-review evidence but MUST NOT be presented as verification of the registry release or its lockfile. The source fixtures can be reproduced without publishing either crate:

No required conformance test may depend on a private repository or private model.

§15 Research basis and extension boundaries

The following are primary research inputs, not implementation or performance claims:

  • Discrete active inference and pymdp: finite-state belief/policy inference and

the pragmatic/epistemic decomposition used by the categorical profile [1–3].

  • AXIOM: object-centric, expanding mixture-based models and sparse interactions;

useful for cheap local structure learning without assuming a giant dense model [4].

  • Scale-free active inference: renormalizing generative models, paths as latent

variables and spatial/temporal hierarchy; a principled extension beyond flat factors [5].

  • Active reasoning: information gain about model hypotheses and Bayesian model

reduction; an extension distinct from state information gain in the core [6].

  • Predictive latent models: V-JEPA 2, LeWorldModel and Dreamer support separate

comparisons of frozen features, compact predictive latents and imagined control; none makes an arbitrary distance-plus-variance objective exact EFE [7–9].

  • Temporal scientific graphs: Hakken motivates time-split relation forecasting

and hypothesis explanations. A predicted relation is not an observed fact [10].

  • Multimodal representations: Qwen3 Embedding, SigLIP 2 and Qwen3-VL Embedding

are candidate frozen encoders; their model-specific revisions, licenses, memory, alignment and end-to-end value must be evaluated rather than assumed [11–13].

Rationale

Exact small categorical factors make the objective independently testable and provide a cheap CPU reference. Local graph work is bounded and explicit instead of hiding an expensive whole-world pass. Learned representations extend the observation space without changing decision or authorization semantics.

Prediction, judgment, routing, language and execution stay separate because each has a different correctness test. The shared contract permits composition: a world model supplies predicted consequences and uncertainty, Kai reads bounded judgments, a solver checks hard constraints, Enso budgets authorized compute and Zen explains the actual trace. A single language model is not required to play all five roles.

The specification names approximation and incompleteness instead of treating confidence as knowledge. Gradients, Bayesian updates, preference changes and permission changes are different operations, so a useful learning loop cannot silently become an authority-escalation loop.

Security Considerations

Tenant/world/model/feature-cache keys MUST include the authenticated scope; an untrusted body/header MUST NOT select another tenant's capabilities. Hosts MUST strip caller-controlled trusted identity headers. World reads, trace retrieval, training captures and artifact access require independent scope checks.

Events and assertions are untrusted data. Prompt injection in a document, sensor label, graph edge or generated explanation MUST NOT alter policy, tool permission, artifact paths or executor identity. Replays, conflicting ids, stale snapshots, malformed distributions, unbounded graphs and poisoned observations require the atomicity, validation and finite budgets specified above.

Training poisoning requires provenance, purpose/consent, split barriers, protected evaluations and controlled publication. Adding unspecified noise does not establish differential privacy; signing a poisoned update does not make it correct. A domain capability cannot modify safety policy or learn away a denial.

Traces can reveal sensor locations, business records, personal behavior or model features. Access, retention, deletion and training permissions MUST follow those sources; a public explanation MUST NOT expose private evidence or repository locations. Numerical reproducibility does not grant permission to publish data.
