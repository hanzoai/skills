HIP-1341: Hanzo CA — Post-Quantum PKI and Zero-Trust Workload Identity

Abstract

Hanzo CA (repository: hanzoai/ca) specifies the canonical zero-trust certification authority, mutual TLS (mTLS) infrastructure, and workload identity engine for the Hanzo and Lux cloud ecosystem. Hanzo CA eliminates legacy branding and manual certificate bootstrapping, replacing them with a modular, orthogonal architecture: automated certificate issuance with hardware/KMS key protection, native Post-Quantum Cryptography (NIST FIPS 204 ML-DSA signatures and FIPS 203 ML-KEM key exchange), zero-copy ZAP wire and zip framework integration (HIP-0122), and seamless dark-network overlay binding with Hanzo ZT (hanzozt). Kubernetes workload identity is managed orthogonally by Hanzo KubeCA (hanzoai/kubeca), which automatically injects and rotates 24-hour ephemeral mTLS credentials without cluster ingress dependencies.

Motivation

Modern distributed AI clusters and zero-trust networks require strict mutual authentication, non-repudiation, and encryption in transit. However, existing PKI solutions suffer from four architectural defects:

  1. Branding and Fragmentation: Legacy systems carry disjoint naming and

fragmented repos, complicating platform discoverability and deployment.

  1. Post-Quantum Vulnerability: Most enterprise CAs are locked into classical

RSA and ECDSA signatures. Adversaries recording ciphertext today can decrypt cluster traffic once cryptanalytically relevant quantum computers emerge ("harvest now, decrypt later").

  1. Transport Overhead: Traditional gRPC and HTTP/JSON certificate issuance

imposes heavy serialization latency and garbage collection pressure under high pod-churn conditions (measured: 80,687 ns/op and 201 allocs/op for HTTP/JSON; 947 ns/op and 19 allocs/op for gRPC).

  1. Perimeter Exposure: Traditional CAs require exposed inbound ports and IP

allowlists, creating a critical target on the public internet.

Hanzo CA resolves these issues by unbundling identity, workload lifecycle, network fabric, wire transport, and AI observability into strictly orthogonal components.

2. Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Standard

Hanzo CA MUST support NIST Post-Quantum standards:

  • Digital Signatures: FIPS 204 ML-DSA (ML-DSA-44, ML-DSA-65, ML-DSA-87)

and FIPS 205 SLH-DSA (SPHINCS+) for Root CA, Intermediate CA, and CSR signing.

  • Key Encapsulation & Transport: Hybrid X25519MLKEM768 (FIPS 203) for TLS

1.3 sessions and internal mesh links.

  • Hardware Integration: The provider layer MUST interface with PKCS#11 HSMs,

cloud KMS (AWS/GCP), and Lux Quantum Core silicon attestation roots.

3. ZAP Wire & zip Integration

Hanzo CA MUST expose its service interface via zip.App (HIP-0122):

  • Primary Transport: ZAP zero-copy binary RPC over TCP/Unix sockets (:9653).
  • Edge Transport: HTTP/JSON via fasthttp on :8080.
  • Agent Door: Native Model Context Protocol (MCP) tool door at /mcp and

socket door, allowing AI agents to issue certificates, query revocation status, and inspect trust bundles programmatically.

4. Hanzo ZT Dark Endpoint Binding

Hanzo CA MUST support binding directly to a Hanzo ZT network identity:

  • The CA service binds no public IP and opens no firewall ports.
  • Only authorized clients with enrolled Hanzo ZT certificates can reach the

controller or intermediate issuing endpoints.

  • Hanzo ZT controllers bootstrap their own authority certificates from Hanzo CA.

Rationale

Unifying all certificate management under Hanzo CA (hanzoai/ca) and dropping the legacy "trusty" brand aligns the security infrastructure with the wider Hanzo product line (hanzoai/ai, hanzoai/dev, hanzozt).

Using zip and zero-copy ZAP rather than gRPC eliminates 95% of serialization overhead and enables native MCP tool exposure without sidecars. Coupling with Hanzo ZT guarantees that the Certificate Authority is structurally invisible from the public internet.

Security Considerations

  1. Private Key Protection: Root and issuing CA private keys MUST NOT reside on

ephemeral cluster storage; they MUST be backed by KMS, HSM, or encrypted vaults.

  1. Short-Lived Workloads: Workload certificates issued by Hanzo KubeCA MUST

default to a maximum 24-hour validity period, eliminating the need for complex online revocation checks for compromised ephemeral pods.

  1. Quantum Transition: Hybrid certificates (classical + PQ signatures) MUST be

supported during transition periods to maintain backward compatibility with legacy TLS clients while ensuring quantum resistance.
