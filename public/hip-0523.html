HIP-0523: Channels — One Store, Two Views, Many Bridges

Abstract

A channel is the one place work is discussed. #bugfix-1010 is a channel. A direct message between two people is a channel. A person's thread with an agent is a channel. An Instagram stranger's first message opens a channel. There is no second kind of conversation and no second store holding one.

Its members are of three kinds — human, agent, bot — and the kind changes who does the work, never what the object is. Two surfaces render it: hanzo.team shows the conversation, hanzo.ai shows the same channel as work in progress. Every outside network reaches it through a bridge.

This HIP specifies the primitive, its address, the member kinds, the storage line between an org's channels and a person's, the rule that surfaces never copy from each other, and what a bridge owes. It adds no capability: team (HIP-1048) serves channels at /v1/team/channels (§2.1), and the rest composes what HIP-1066, HIP-1048, HIP-1210, HIP-1107 and HIP-1252 already specify. §13 marks every mechanism it names as shipped, disabled or absent, measured in the source.

Motivation

What this is for: anyone can hold one coherent conversation with anyone — a colleague, a stranger on any network, an agent — and it is the same conversation wherever they open it. Past automation that becomes something better, a place you can watch: channels per problem and per project, swarms visibly working in them, people and agents doing the work side by side. Two faces, one brain — hanzo.ai for a person's own chat and building, hanzo.team for the org's shared space — and both are thin renderings of one cloud, not two products with a pipe between them.

What stands in the way is that four things in this estate already hold a conversation and none of them can see the others. apps/transport holds an inbox of messages that arrived from Slack, Discord, Teams and Telegram. apps/team holds channels and chat messages, one SQLite per (org, workspace). apps/agents delegates its threads to an external module. apps/help holds a support thread as a framework document. A question asked in Slack, answered by a person in the team workspace, escalated to a ticket and finished by an agent crosses four stores and arrives as four unrelated records. Nobody can read the sequence back, because no store holds it.

The cost is not tidiness. It is that the agent which could do the work is a member of none of them, so each subsystem grew its own answer to "may a model reply here" — apps/transport has a turn, apps/team has a responder — and the two now answer the same message twice or not at all. HIP-1066's Motivation records the first version of that defect; the estate has since reproduced it across capabilities rather than inside one. One object with one member list ends it: the agent is a member because everyone in the conversation is a member, and there is nothing else to configure.

Specification

The key words MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT and MAY are to be interpreted as in RFC 2119.

§1 The primitive is the channel

A channel is the conversation a message lives in. It is the word people already say for it, the word hanzo.team draws above it, and the class the team store keeps it under (team:class:Channel). One word, from the screen to the store.

The network a message travels over has been called a channel too. It is a transport, and in this HIP channel never means it:

| Word | What it means | Owner | |---|---|---| | channel | the conversation a message lives in, kinds dm, group, thread | this HIP | | transport | the network a message travels over — slack, discord, teams, telegram for conversation; X, Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn for publishing | HIP-1066, HIP-1153 |

HIP-1066's envelope carries both: the transport as Message.Transport and the channel as Message.Channel, at /v1/transport (apps/transport/envelope.go). Both moved in the same wave as §2.1, so no field anywhere means the conversation by one name and the network by the other.

§2 The channel

A channel is an ordered, durable sequence of envelopes (HIP-1066) plus a member list plus a name. It has exactly these properties and no others:

  • A scope — an org or a person (§5). The scope decides where it is stored.
  • Members — one or more, of the kinds in §3.
  • A life — standing or bound. A standing channel persists until deleted.

A bound channel names one work object and archives when that object closes.

  • Bindings — zero or more references to work objects: a project, a

repository, an issue. A binding is a REFERENCE, never a copy. HIP-1160 §1 makes the issue the one work-item primitive and forbids a parallel store; a channel holding an issue's title or status would be exactly that store. A surface showing a bound issue reads it through todo at render time.

  • An origin — native, or the bridge that opened it (§7).

There is no separate object for a direct message, a group, a work channel or an agent thread. Those are a channel's member count and its kind.

§2.1 The addresses

team (HIP-1048) serves channels under /v1/team/channels. Every operation is typed (HIP-0106) and reads the one store of §6.

| Operation | What it does | |---|---| | GET /v1/team/channels | the channels the caller may see, each with its kind, life and bindings | | POST /v1/team/channels | opens a channel | | PUT /v1/team/channels/{id} | states its life and bindings | | PATCH /v1/team/channels/{id} | renames it, sets its topic, archives it | | GET /v1/team/channels/{id}/members | its roster | | POST /v1/team/channels/{id}/members | adds members | | DELETE /v1/team/channels/{id}/members/{account} | removes one | | GET /v1/team/channels/{id}/messages | its history | | POST /v1/team/channels/{id}/messages | sends to it | | POST /v1/team/dms | opens the direct channel between the people named, or answers the one that exists |

Every body and answer says channel. A list is {"channels": […]}, one is {"channel": {…}}, and a message names the channel it is in as channel. A channel's kind on this surface is public, private or dm. The work properties (life and bindings) are stored directly as top-level attributes on the channel document. The SDK carries the same nouns — Channel, useChannels, useMessages, open (HIP-0528) — and so does every projection of the served document: the tool, the CLI, the generated clients.

There is no second spelling. /v1/team/rooms answers 404 like any address nothing serves.

§3 Members: human, agent, bot

One member model, three kinds. The kind says what runs when the member is addressed:

| Kind | What it is | Where it runs | |---|---|---| | human | a person, identified by the IAM principal (HIP-0026) | nowhere | | agent | an agent definition, addressed on demand | a session, per HIP-1210 | | bot | a standing presence that is always resident | a run, per HIP-1107 |

An agent member is dormant until a message addresses it. Addressing it starts a session; the session ends; the membership does not. A bot member is resident whether or not anyone is talking to it — the Slack presence is one — and the thing behind the seat is a HIP-1107 bot run. This HIP mints no fourth meaning of "bot." HIP-1107 §3 names three values for that word; the member kind here is a SEAT in a channel, and the run occupying the seat is HIP-1107's.

Membership is authorization. HIP-1048 already states this for a workspace — "the membership rows ARE the authorization" — and this HIP extends that roster to channels rather than introducing a second one. A reader MUST NOT find two places that answer "who is in here".

§4 Two views of one channel

Two surfaces render the same channel from the same store. Neither owns it.

  • hanzo.team — the conversation view. The org's shared space: the channel

list, the message history, the members, the calls, the virtual offices.

  • hanzo.ai — the work view. The same channel shown as what is happening in

it: the agent sessions under way, the code they are touching, the transcript, a live preview of the result.

Same channel, same history, same members, different rendering. A message sent from either appears in both because both read one store, not because anything reconciles them. meet (HIP-1252) and todo (HIP-1160) render inside both views; neither is a third surface with its own copy.

A view is not a build. Both views come from one codebase, and HIP-0504 owns that rule: one substrate (@hanzo/gui) and one component library per brand (@hanzo/ui) across web, native and desktop. So hanzo.ai in a browser, Hanzo Desktop and Hanzo Mobile are one surface on three hosts rather than three implementations of it — a channel renders the same in all three because it is the same code reading the same store. Note the direction HIP-0504 requires: an app imports its brand's ui and never the substrate directly, which it calls non-conformant. "One codebase" is therefore a statement about the substrate underneath, not a licence for an app to reach past its own library.

§5 Two scopes, one mechanism, and the tenancy line is the storage line

A channel belongs to an org. That is the whole rule, and a person's own channels are not an exception to it.

Every person has a home org, and not by convention: a user's id is <owner>/<name> and the owner half IS an org, so a resolved principal always carries one (HIP-0026). One human may belong to many orgs, and exactly one of them is their home. So the two scopes are two ADDRESSES on one mechanism, never two mechanisms:

  • Org scope — a shared org's channels and their whole history, canonical in

that org's store. Today that is apps/team's per-(org, workspace) SQLite (apps/team/store.go), which already holds channels, direct messages and chat messages. hanzo.ai READS this store; it MUST NOT keep a copy.

  • Personal scope — the same mechanism at the person's own org: their threads

with their agents, and anything addressed to them rather than to an employer.

The isolation is therefore free, and no query has to remember it. Work channels sit in the work org's file and personal channels in the person's own org's file because per-org storage already puts them in different files. A colleague cannot read a personal channel for exactly the reason one org cannot read another's — not because a predicate excluded them. A shared store with an is_personal column would be the same fact one bug away from an org admin reading their reports' direct messages, and this design never writes that column.

This is the same line HIP-1065 draws for credentials: "which accounts has this ORGANIZATION connected" is an administrative fact, and "which accounts has THIS PERSON linked" is the person's own property with no admin gate on it. A conversation is at least as personal as a token.

A person's own org is an ordinary org and gets no special case anywhere. It is smaller, and that is the only difference. Anything that would need to ask "is this the personal one" has reintroduced the predicate this section removes.

Promotion is a copy, never a move: a person MAY post a personal channel's content into an org channel, and that act creates new envelopes in the org store with the person as sender. Nothing is silently reclassified.

§6 One store, N views, M bridges

Surfaces MUST NOT synchronize with each other. There is no hanzo.team → hanzo.ai sync, in either direction, ever. Two live views over one store cannot disagree; two stores kept in step always eventually do, and the estate has paid for that lesson three times — HIP-1107 §1 keeps no second copy of a run for it, HIP-1160 §1 forbids mirroring the forge for it, and HIP-1252's Rationale refuses to hold membership of its own for it.

The shape is fixed:

                    external network ──┐
                                       ├─ bridge ─┐
                    external network ──┘          │
                                                  ▼
    hanzo.team ────── view ──────────────▶  THE CHANNEL STORE
    hanzo.ai   ────── view ──────────────▶  (org scope | personal scope)

A view holds no messages. A bridge holds no messages. Exactly one store does.

One consequence is immediate and is a defect today: the store must hold both halves of every conversation. apps/transport writes an arriving message to channel_inbox and a sent one to channel_send, and the two are different tables (apps/transport/store.go:109,126) — so no single read returns the conversation. A channel is the sequence, so a channel store that records only what arrived is not one.

§7 A bridge

A bridge joins one external network to the channel store. Every bridge owes exactly four things, and a transport is not connected until it has all four:

  1. A binding table — our channel ↔ theirs, both directions, so either side

can be resolved from the other. Creation is bridged both ways: a channel created on an outside network with our presence in it opens a channel here, and a channel created here with that transport bound opens one there.

  1. A relay — messages move in both directions as they happen, normalized

into and rendered out of HIP-1066's envelope. A bridge MUST NOT invent a second message shape.

  1. Echo suppression — a message this estate sent and the network echoed back

MUST NOT re-enter the channel. Without it every reply appears twice and every agent answers its own message.

  1. Identity mapping — external account ↔ our principal, so a bridged sender

is a member and not an anonymous string. HIP-1066 already places this question in integrations: which Hanzo account a chat user has linked is that package's to answer, "only the answer crosses. A token never does."

Credentials are not the bridge's. They live sealed in the key store under a path built from the tenant, reached through integrations (HIP-1250) or connectors (HIP-1065). HIP-1066's one-way rule holds for channels too: transport and channels depend on integrations; integrations MUST NOT depend on either.

A bot is not a bridge. A bot may be a member of a bridged channel, and the channel still relays with no bot present. Any transport that only works when a bot is resident has been built wrong.

§8 Transports, and what each one honestly costs

HIP-1066's transport registry is closed and enumerated — four members today (apps/transport/registry.go:76). A new transport is a tagged member added to that registry declaring what it can actually render — direct messages, group channels, threading — and those declarations are honest rather than aspirational. It is not a new subsystem.

The roster, with the real remaining cost of each stated rather than implied:

| Transport | State | What is actually left to do | |---|---|---| | slack, discord, teams, telegram | in the registry | bind them to the channel store; today they reach an inbox, not a channel. Discord carries no direct message and says so | | whatsapp, imessage, signal, and ~20 more | adapter exists, elsewhere | @hanzo/bot already speaks these (bot/src/channels/registry.ts, plus extensions); apps/integrations holds only a WhatsApp credential connector. The work is a registry member and a relay, not an adapter | | email | send-only | outbound connectors exist (apps/integrations/messaging.go); HIP-0061 owns /send/email. Nothing binds an arriving mail to a channel | | sms, voice | send-only | tel (HIP-1069) owns numbers, calls and messages and is not a registry member | | x, linkedin, facebook, instagram | publish-only | social (HIP-1153) publishes to these; publishing is not conversation. Messaging on the Meta networks is a new adapter with its own review and its own reply-window rules |

Two of these deserve their true cost said out loud. Publishing to a network is not a conversation with it — sharing an account's credential does not give inbound messages, and four rows above are publish-only for that reason. iMessage has no server API. It needs a relay on Apple hardware holding a real account, which is an operational and policy commitment before it is an engineering one. @hanzo/bot reaches it by exactly that route. Do not list it beside the others as though it were an adapter.

§9 The hub channel and the correspondent channel

Each connected transport gets one hub channel — #whatsapp, #instagram — standing, org-scoped, whose members are the people and agents who work that network. The hub is where that network's outbound work is done: a post is drafted, discussed and published from it through social (HIP-1153), which remains the one publisher.

Each inbound conversation from an outside person opens one correspondent channel, named #<transport>-dm-<their-handle>, created on the first message with no setup. It is a child of the hub, org-scoped, standing, and visible in both views. A reply sent from any surface leaves on the transport it arrived on, as the org's identity there.

A correspondent channel is a shared inbox: any member may answer, and members include agents. There is no assignment step and no owner column. Handling is whoever picks it up, which is the property that makes a channel worth having instead of a queue.

Two limits are not negotiable. Auto-creation is bounded — an unbounded channel per inbound stranger is a channel-creation flood with a name attached — so it is subject to the access decision HIP-1066 already makes per (org, transport): pairing, allowlist, or open. And HIP-1066's rule that sender identifiers MUST NOT appear in logs holds here too; the channel's name carries the correspondent's handle, and the channel is not a log.

§10 The ambient agent

The org's agent is a member of every new channel, by default, as org policy.

This is one line in one function. There MUST be exactly one place a channel is created, and default membership is applied there. Applied anywhere else it is a policy with holes: a channel made by a bridge, by an agent, or by a person would each get their own answer. That single place does not exist today (§13), and creating it is the prerequisite for this section, not a detail of it.

The agent is addressed by mention and runs a session (HIP-1210). It holds no authority a member does not have: it reads the channel it is in, and no other.

First contact with an outside person is answered by a human. What the agent MAY do without review is recurrence — an answer a person already approved, asked again — and the authority a specific member has to send without a person's act is specified per user in HIP-0524, not here.

One responder, not two. apps/transport' turn and apps/team's responder are two implementations of "a model answers a message", and with one channel store they answer the same message. They MUST fold to one, and the survivor is the one beside the store that holds the message, for the reason HIP-1066 already gives: everything the turn needs — the policy, the reply route, the egress — is already there.

§11 A swarm is a session tree

Fanning out is not a new mechanism. An orchestrating agent starts child sessions under a root; the children are research, coding, browser or computer work; the tree is the record. HIP-1210 owns the session, its events and its per-org store, and the tree is already served.

The work view (§4) renders that tree live: what each session is doing, and the controls to pause, resume, stop or message any node of it. Compute under a session is a sandbox lease (HIP-1146) — one primitive, whatever the lifetime.

The point of rendering it is not display. It is that a person can see and steer every piece of agentic work from the channel the work was asked for in, rather than learning where each subsystem hid its own console.

§12 A call happens in the channel

A call is not a separate place. meet (HIP-1252) mints a join token for a channel's call, and holds no membership of its own: it asks the roster who is in.

Three consequences follow, and each is a constraint rather than a feature:

  1. The join decision still asks the roster. meet asks team who is a

member; with channels it asks the channel's member list. It MUST NOT grow a second membership store, which is precisely the design HIP-1252's Rationale rejects.

  1. An agent that joins a call is a participant with an identity and a seat.

meet stamps identity server-side and debits per seat. An agent listener is therefore a named participant and a billed one; there is no free silent observer, and a spec claiming otherwise would be claiming a meet that does not exist.

  1. The summary is a participant's work, not the token minter's. Media rides

browser-to-server and never passes through the API binary, so nothing in the join decision can see a word that was said. The path is: a recording participant receives the media, transcription runs over it, and the summary is posted into the channel as ordinary envelopes — read in the conversation view, streamed into the work view. The recording half of that already exists (apps/meet/record.go, apps/meet/egress.go); transcription and the posting step do not (§13). Note that HIP-1252 §1 says recording "is either the media server's or nobody's" while apps/meet drives it; that is a drift between the spec and the code and it is HIP-1252's to resolve, not this HIP's to restate.

Two corrections to beliefs that were in circulation while this was written, both found by reading the source. The bot-member projection is not uncalled — it reconciles on every transactor connect; what is uncalled is the channel-level projection, which is a different function in a different service. And senderUser is not unpopulated on one path; it is unpopulated on every path, by construction, because identity resolution was deliberately removed from ingest.

§14 Conformance

An implementation conforms when all of these hold:

  1. One store answers "what was said in this channel", holding both halves of the

conversation, and no surface holds a second copy.

  1. Org channels and personal channels are in different stores, not separated by

a predicate.

  1. Exactly one function creates a channel, and default membership is applied

there.

  1. Exactly one responder decides whether a model answers a message.
  2. Every bridge has all four properties of §7. A transport with three of them is

not connected, and MUST NOT be listed as though it were.

  1. Every stored message carries a resolved sender, or is refused. A member list

over unresolved identifiers is not a member list.

  1. A channel's binding to a work object resolves through todo; no channel row

carries an issue's title, state or assignee.

  1. Every address, body, stored name and SDK noun for the conversation says

channel (§2.1). No second spelling is served.

Rationale

The alternative is what exists: each surface owning its own conversation store and a synchronizer between them. It is the obvious design, because each surface can then ship alone, and it is wrong for a reason that shows up only later. Two stores kept in step have two answers to "what was said", and they disagree under exactly the conditions nobody tests — a partition, a redelivery, a message edited on one side. One store with two views cannot have that class of bug at all, because there is no second copy to disagree.

The alternative to one member model is a per-surface participant type, which is how the estate got two responders. The moment "who may reply" is answered twice, one of the answers is stale.

The alternative to channel is room, which kept the conversation clear of the transport and the social account by giving it a word nobody says. That made every layer translate: people said channel, the screen drew channels, the store's class was Channel, and only the wire and the SDK said room. One word per object, used from the screen to the store, is cheaper than a private word, and the network a message travels over keeps a word of its own: transport (§1). Renaming without an alias is the same choice HIP-0139 makes for an address: two spellings of one thing double every table the surface flows into.

Security Considerations

The scope line is the isolation boundary. Org channels and personal channels in one store make privacy a query predicate. Any defect in that predicate — a missing clause, a join, an admin surface that forgets it — reads a person's direct messages to their manager. Separate stores make the same defect impossible to write.

A bridge is an identity translator, and translators forge. A bridged message arrives claiming an external sender; the mapping in §7 turns that claim into a member. An unverified mapping lets anyone who can post on the external network speak in the channel as a member. The mapping MUST be resolved by integrations, which holds the link, and MUST NOT be inferred from a display name. Note that this is not hypothetical today: the stored inbox row's senderUser is always empty (§13), so anything that promotes a stored row to a member would be promoting an unauthenticated external string.

Auto-created channels are an unauthenticated write. §9 opens a channel on a stranger's message. Without the access decision it inherits from HIP-1066, that is unbounded creation of named objects by anyone who can find the org's account on any connected network.

The ambient agent is a member of everything, which makes it the widest principal in the estate. It MUST hold only what a member holds. An agent that reads across channels because it is convenient is a cross-channel read with a friendly name, and in a bridged channel the party on the other end is not an employee.

Attribution is a security property, not a courtesy. A message an agent sent MUST be recorded as agent-sent. A channel whose history cannot distinguish a person's words from a model's words cannot be used as evidence of anything, which is the whole reason to keep the history.

Echo suppression is a safety limit, not tidiness. Without it, an agent answering a bridged channel can answer its own relayed reply. Two such channels bridged to each other do it without stopping.

The rename leaves no reachable old name. /v1/team/rooms stops answering in the wave that serves /v1/team/channels; nothing forwards it. A forwarding alias would be a second address whose authorization has to be kept identical to the first by hand.
